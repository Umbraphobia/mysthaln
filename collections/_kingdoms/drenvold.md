Drenvold is the storm-lashed homeland of the **Thaldrim**, a race of sea-dwarves whose lives are bound to both forge and tide. Carved into the cliffs and fjords of Morwen’s northeastern coast, Drenvold is a land where the sea is both master and muse. Its people have learned to temper iron with saltwater, and courage with hardship. Great **Seahearths**—half-city, half-forge—cling to the rock like barnacles of civilization, each one a testament to endurance against the roaring deep.

Though proud and fiercely independent, the Thaldrim remember the ancient unity of their kind and the great voyages that once linked the far coasts of Mysthaln. Today, Drenvold stands as both a maritime power and a bastion of tradition, watching warily as the fires of the Supremacy burn ever closer beyond the mountains.

---

## History

Drenvold’s history begins in the **Age of Founding**, when the Thaldrim broke away from their stonebound cousins and followed the call of the sea. Legends speak of **Drenmir the Deep Forger**, who struck the ocean’s heart with his hammer and shaped the first Seahearth from the molten cliffs. From that act came both their name and their creed: *“The sea yields only to those who strike true.”*

For centuries, Drenvold’s people traded across the northern waters, establishing colonies and trading halls from the Supremacy’s coast to the far isles. Their greatest colony was **Skarhold**, once a proud outpost and now a symbol of loss—taken generations ago by foreign rulers.

The rise of the **Supremacy** brought war to their southern shores. When the Empire’s legions sought to breach the mountains, the Drenvoldic fleets blockaded the coast and held the passes, turning their homeland into a fortress-state. Though the great war subsided, the memory of invasion never did. To this day, the Thaldrim keep watch from their storm towers, their loyalty to freedom hardened like iron in brine.

---

## Geography

Drenvold’s landscape is a study in stone and sea. Towering cliffs plunge into gray waters, their faces riddled with tunnels and docks carved by generations of artisans. The **Stormbarrier Range** to the southwest shields Drenvold from the Supremacy’s reach, while the western border with **Njorath** fades into frigid tundra and broken icefields.

Its capital, **Karrhold**, rises from a crescent-shaped bay guarded by colossal stone breakwaters. Inland, geothermal vents warm valleys where hardy mosses and fungus farms sustain the population. The north and east coasts are dotted with narrow fjords, while deep volcanic caverns provide both warmth and ore for their legendary forges.

---

## Economy

Drenvold’s economy is built upon three pillars: **forging, shipbuilding, and trade**. Thaldrim blacksmiths are said to forge metal that sings when struck, and their sea-tempered steel is prized across Mysthaln. Shipwrights of Drenvold craft vessels capable of enduring the world’s harshest seas, blending magic and engineering in seamless harmony.

The Seahearths operate as independent economic centers, each specializing in different crafts—armor from Grimvein, engines from Brodun, ship hulls from Dravhild, and enchanted metalwork from Kraesund. While gold and silver exist, **iron, salt, and coral** serve as common measures of wealth.

Though Drenvold trades freely with Vardessa and Njorath, relations with the Supremacy remain hostile. The mountain passes are heavily fortified, and maritime patrols regularly intercept Supremacy vessels that drift too close to their waters.

---

## Demographics

Drenvold’s population is approximately **160,000**, with **Thaldrim** making up the overwhelming majority. A small number of **Draenfyr** and **Humans** live in the larger ports, mostly as traders or scholars. The Thaldrim population is divided among 24 major settlements, with **Karrhold** as the capital and **Vandruk**, **Thrunhal**, and **Grimvein** serving as key industrial centers.

Clan identity remains central to Thaldrim life. Each clan controls part of a Seahearth, contributing both warriors and craftsmen to its upkeep. Loyalty to clan and Seahearth outweighs loyalty to king or council—but when Drenvold is threatened, its people unite under the **Deep Banner**, the symbol of Drenmir’s hammer over the sea.

---

## Culture

To the Thaldrim, **the sea is a forge**, and life is the act of tempering the soul against its storms. Their culture values endurance, craft, and camaraderie, expressed through roaring feasts, ritual duels, and deep reverence for the bond between maker and made. Music is percussive and rhythmic, echoing the beat of hammers on anvils and waves on cliffs.

The Drenvoldic religion centers on **Drenmir the Deep Forger**, god of tides, creation, and transformation. Offerings—usually a drop of blood or a shard of iron—are cast into the sea before voyages or great undertakings. The Thaldrim believe the sea tests all things, wearing away the false until only truth remains.

Art and architecture are practical yet beautiful: glowing forge halls lined with runes, ships adorned with carvings of sea monsters, and beacons lit with blue flame to guide sailors home. Their festivals, such as the **Hammerwake** and **Night of Chains**, honor both ancestors and the ocean’s power, blending solemn ritual with fierce joy.
